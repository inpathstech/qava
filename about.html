Why I started this

Idea-to-done, in our AI world.

I've spent 12 years watching people bet on silver bullets. The product will go live on TikTok. Nobody will challenge the reorg. It'll sell because it's a good product. I've been in the rooms where those assumptions got made and billion-dollar valuations went vertical on the back of them, and I've seen what happens when they finally get tested and don't stand up.

Right now the assumption is AI. It'll build the product, design the strategy, run the business, answer the customers. I think it has every hallmark of the ones before it. That doesn't mean AI isn't useful — I use it constantly and it's taken the grind out of half my week. It just doesn't do everything for you.

I started The Club to fill that gap:

  • How-to's, so you know what AI is actually doing for you and can steer it
  • A discussion board, where people connect and collaborate
  • A job board, for short projects, full-time roles, and internships

After the pilot, and seeing the results, there was no question this was what I wanted to build. I'm glad you're here. I hope you join, and I hope you double down on the thing you're actually after — more money, your life's work, whatever it is for you.

Reed Langridge

The Club, New York

What I believe

Three things I keep coming back to.

Life is short, and everyone can be earning more.

Everyone has something to offer and their own particular skills. But nobody is good at everything. What moves things forward is AI for the parts it handles well, and collaboration with sharp people for the rest.

If it were easy, someone would have done it already.

Good strategic work takes time, and it stays hard. You can use AI to accelerate a great deal of it, and you should. But human intuition is irreplaceable, and pretending otherwise is how good calls get missed.

People are closer than they think to what's next.

Most people are one framework and one honest conversation away from what they want, whether that's an idea out in the real world or a better career. The distance is shorter than it looks from where you stand.

How it works

Pick your side of the table.

Both sides join the same membership. What happens next is different.

What you get

+Business-trained, motivated people on a scoped project
+A brief that defines the outcome before anyone starts
+Frameworks your team can reuse after the project ends
+A faster read on fit than a hiring process gives you

What you won't get

−An expensive open-ended retainer
−A junior body billed as a strategist
−A deck with no decision in it

The sequence

01Tell me the decision you're stuck on — not the task you want done.
02We scope it together into something deliverable in weeks, not quarters.
03It goes to the board, or to specific members I think can do it.
04You pick. Work starts against an agreed outcome and a date.
05You keep the output, the framework, and the option to rehire.
