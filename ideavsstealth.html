What mode are you?

The journey begins long before the world sees it

When a startup (or a project within a company) is operating in stealth mode, it's deliberately staying under the radar—keeping its product, strategy, team, and progress secret until it's ready to unveil. We respect this approach: if you're building something special and not yet ready to reveal details, it's perfectly acceptable to simply say your company is "in stealth mode."

Why companies go stealth:

  • Protect intellectual property: Prevent rivals from copying your ideas before you've secured patents or a first-mover advantage.
  • Control the narrative: Maintain focus, avoid hype, and reserve public-facing messaging until launch.
  • Avoid premature attention: Keep things quiet to deter competitors or shutter noise until you're pressure-tested and polished.

Idea mode is the initial phase when all you really have is a concept—no product, no team, maybe not even a business. It's that exciting but raw stage where you're building the foundation: validating assumptions, understanding who your customers are, testing hypotheses, sketching rudimentary financial models, and scoping out the competition.

What happens during idea mode:

  • Customer segmentation: Who will use/buy this?
  • Problem validation: Do they actually have the problem you want to solve?
  • Competitor analysis: Who else is doing something similar? Where's the gap?
  • Financial modeling: What costs, pricing strategy, revenue streams make sense?
  • Early prototypes or sketches: A few slides or clickable screens to start testing concept
